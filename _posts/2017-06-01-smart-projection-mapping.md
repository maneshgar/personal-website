Spatial augmented reality, better known as **projection mapping**, turns a
real-world object or scene into a surface for video projection. Disney pioneered
it in the seventies, but it has become popular only recently, as specialised
software made the otherwise cumbersome calibration much easier. Today it's used
in advertising, cultural events, live performances and theatre to enhance an
object or scene with projected visual content.

My master's thesis at the Immersive and Creative Technologies (ICT) Lab at
Concordia University, *Spatial Augmented Reality in Outdoor Areas Using
Long-Range Vision Systems*, took this outdoors and over long distances.

The system automatically adjusts the projected content to compensate for any
adverse behaviour of the building surfaces, using dense 3D reconstruction and
estimating the reflectance properties of the building's facade.

This work led to two papers, at GRAPP 2017 and ACM Multimedia 2017.
