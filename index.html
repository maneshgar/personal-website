About me

I am a machine learning researcher whose work spans machine vision, autonomous driving, and computational biology. My background combines academic research with industry experience, and I work at the intersection of these fields, which are becoming more closely connected every day.

My path in computer science began in 2010, when I enrolled in the Software Engineering program at Isfahan University in Iran. After graduating in 2014, I moved to Canada to pursue a master’s degree in computer science at Concordia University. There, I worked with Dr. Sudhir P. Mudur and Dr. Charalambos Poullis in the Immersive and Creative Technologies Lab, where I deepened my expertise in computer vision.

After my master’s, I joined Matrox Electronic Systems as a software designer, at a time when deep learning was first moving from research into industry. Over four years, I applied it to real machine vision problems, from segmentation to inspection, and led a team in prototyping machine learning solutions for use in the field. In 2021, I moved to Vancouver to join Huawei Technologies, where I worked on autonomous driving across perception, prediction, and planning.

Over time, I became interested in biology and how AI could help us better understand biological systems and human health. In 2024, I returned to academia to pursue a PhD in Bioinformatics at the University of British Columbia, under the supervision of Dr. Ali Bashashati at the AIMLab. My research brings my machine learning background to questions in human biology, starting with foundation models for gene expression data.

My journey is still unfolding, and I try to keep this website up to date as it does. Thanks for stopping by!
